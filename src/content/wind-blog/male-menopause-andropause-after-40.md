Men in their 40s and 50s sometimes notice changes in energy, mood, strength, sleep or sexual interest. It is common to hear these changes described as "male menopause". The phrase is familiar, but it can give the wrong impression.

Men do not usually experience a sudden, universal hormonal transition equivalent to women's menopause. Testosterone levels tend to decline gradually with age, and the pace varies considerably between individuals. Some men develop clinically low testosterone with symptoms; many do not.

## What is andropause?

Andropause is an informal term sometimes used for age-related changes in male hormones. Doctors may instead discuss testosterone deficiency or hypogonadism when a man has compatible symptoms and consistently low testosterone levels.

A diagnosis should not be made from age or symptoms alone. Tiredness, low mood, reduced sexual interest and loss of strength can have many causes.

## What symptoms might be involved?

Possible symptoms of testosterone deficiency include reduced libido, fewer spontaneous erections, erectile difficulties, reduced muscle mass, increased body fat, low energy, low mood and reduced bone density. These symptoms are not specific to testosterone deficiency.

Sleep apnoea, obesity, diabetes, thyroid conditions, depression, alcohol use, some medicines and chronic illness can also affect energy, sexual function and hormone levels.

## How is testosterone deficiency assessed?

A clinician will consider symptoms, medical history, medicines, sleep, weight and other possible causes. Diagnosis generally requires testosterone testing on more than one occasion, using appropriately timed morning blood samples and interpreted in the context of the laboratory's reference range and the person's clinical picture.

A single low result during illness, poor sleep or another temporary stressor may not represent a persistent deficiency. Further tests may be needed to understand whether the problem relates to the testes, pituitary gland or another cause.

## Does every man need testosterone treatment after 40?

No. Testosterone therapy is not an anti-ageing treatment for every man and should not be started simply because someone is getting older or feels tired.

For men with confirmed deficiency, a specialist may discuss possible benefits and risks. Treatment requires monitoring. Testosterone can affect fertility and may not be appropriate for men with certain medical conditions. Men who wish to have children should discuss fertility before considering treatment.

Lifestyle changes may help when low testosterone is associated with obesity, poor sleep, inactivity or other reversible health factors. These changes are not a guaranteed substitute for treatment in men with an established hormonal disorder.

## The Indian context

Diabetes, excess weight, sleep apnoea and cardiovascular disease are important health issues for many Indian men in midlife. They can overlap with symptoms sometimes attributed to low testosterone. A proper medical assessment is more useful than buying a testosterone booster online or relying on a single test.

Be cautious about supplements and products promising to raise testosterone naturally. Claims may be stronger than the evidence, and products can contain undeclared ingredients or interact with medicines.

## The takeaway

Male midlife hormonal health deserves attention, but "male menopause" is not a diagnosis. Persistent sexual, mood, sleep or energy changes deserve a proper assessment that looks at the whole person rather than assuming testosterone is the answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every man experience andropause after 40?**

No. Testosterone changes vary, and not every man develops clinically significant deficiency.

**Can low testosterone cause tiredness?**

It can be one possible contributor, but tiredness has many other causes, including poor sleep, anaemia, thyroid problems, diabetes, depression and medication effects.

**Can I take testosterone boosters without a test?**

It is not advisable to self-treat. Discuss symptoms and testing with a qualified clinician before taking hormone products or supplements.
