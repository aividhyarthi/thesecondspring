# Can a Digital Symptom Tracker Help You Understand Perimenopause?

A woman may notice that her sleep is worse in the week before her period, that headaches arrive at unexpected times or that her cycle has become less predictable. When these changes happen over several months, it can be difficult to explain the pattern during a short medical appointment.

A symptom diary or digital tracker may help organise those observations. But can an app actually identify perimenopause?

A study published online in *Menopause* on 10 November 2026 evaluated a digital Perimenopause Symptom Scale, known as Peri-SS. The research offers useful information about measuring symptoms. It does not show that an app can diagnose perimenopause on its own.

## What did the researchers test?

The researchers evaluated the Peri-SS among 1,255 women in the United States aged 35 to 59. Of these participants, 633 self-identified as perimenopausal.

Participants completed the Peri-SS, the Menopause Rating Scale and a quality-of-life questionnaire at baseline and again 7 to 14 days later. The researchers assessed whether the scale measured symptoms consistently and whether it related to other measures in expected ways.

The Peri-SS covered four domains:

- Vasomotor symptoms, such as hot flashes and night sweats.
- Psychological symptoms.
- Sexual symptoms.
- Physical symptoms.

The researchers reported good internal consistency and test-retest reliability. The overall internal consistency value was 0.90, and the test-retest reliability value was 0.82. Peri-SS scores were also strongly correlated with Menopause Rating Scale scores.

These findings suggest that the questionnaire performed well in the population studied.

## What does a symptom scale actually do?

A symptom scale gives a person a structured way to record and describe what she is experiencing. It can make a conversation with a clinician more specific.

For example, instead of saying, "I feel different these days", a woman may be able to describe:

- How often she experiences night sweats.
- Whether sleep problems happen throughout the month or around particular cycle changes.
- Whether mood symptoms affect work or daily life.
- Whether physical discomfort is becoming more frequent.
- Whether symptoms have changed since the previous month.

A structured record can help a clinician understand the pattern. It does not establish the cause of every symptom.

## What the study does not prove

There are important limits.

**It was not a diagnostic accuracy study.** The study evaluated the measurement properties of a symptom scale. It did not establish that the questionnaire can independently diagnose perimenopause or distinguish it reliably from every other condition.

**The participants were in the United States.** The study does not establish that the scale performs in the same way among Indian women, whose languages, health experiences and symptom descriptions may differ.

**Some participants were recruited through an online research platform.** The sample should not be treated as representative of all women in the United States, let alone India.

**The study has industry connections.** Several authors were employees of Flo Health and held equity interests in the company. One author was a consultant to Flo Health. This does not automatically invalidate the findings, but readers should be able to see the disclosed relationships and interpret the study accordingly.

## Could Indian women use a symptom diary?

A simple diary can be useful even without a specialised app. A woman may choose to note:

- First day of each period and any unusual bleeding.
- Sleep quality and night-time waking.
- Hot flashes and night sweats.
- Mood changes and anxiety.
- Headaches, joint discomfort or other symptoms.
- Medicines and supplements being taken.
- The effect of symptoms on work, relationships and daily activities.

The purpose is to notice patterns and communicate clearly with a healthcare professional. It is not to score yourself or decide that a symptom must be hormonal.

If using an app, read its privacy policy before entering menstrual or health information. Check what data it collects, how it is stored, whether it is shared and how account data can be deleted. Avoid assuming that a commercial app is equivalent to a medical record or a clinical diagnosis.

## Why local validation matters

A questionnaire developed and evaluated in one country may not automatically work equally well in another. Language translation alone is not enough. Researchers need to test whether questions are understood in the same way, whether the scale covers relevant experiences and whether its measurement properties remain reliable in the new population.

For India, validation would ideally include women from different regions, language groups, rural and urban settings, and income levels. Researchers should also explain how participants' reproductive stages were determined.

The November study is a useful starting point for digital symptom measurement. It is not evidence that this particular scale is ready for clinical use among Indian women.

## How to prepare for a medical appointment

If symptoms are affecting daily life, a short record over several weeks may help you explain what is happening. Take note of changes in periods, the symptoms that trouble you most, when they occur and any medicines you use.

A clinician may consider perimenopause alongside other possible explanations. Depending on the symptoms and history, these may include thyroid conditions, anaemia, pregnancy, sleep disorders, medication effects or other health conditions.

Do not delay medical assessment for very heavy bleeding, bleeding after sex, persistent bleeding between periods or bleeding after menopause.

## The takeaway

Digital tools may help women record symptoms and communicate patterns. The 2026 Peri-SS study found encouraging measurement results in a US sample, but it did not establish that an app can diagnose perimenopause or that the scale is validated for Indian women.

Use a diary as a conversation aid, not as a diagnosis. The next research step is to test symptom measures across different populations, languages and healthcare settings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an app tell me that I am in perimenopause?

An app can help record symptoms and menstrual patterns. It cannot independently confirm perimenopause or rule out other causes.

### How long should I track symptoms?

There is no single required duration for every woman. Recording symptoms and menstrual changes over several weeks may help you describe patterns at an appointment. Seek care sooner if symptoms are concerning.

### Is the Peri-SS validated for Indian women?

The November 2026 study evaluated the scale among US women. The study does not establish validation in Indian populations.

### Should I share menstrual data with a health app?

Only after understanding its privacy policy, data collection, sharing practices and deletion options. Menstrual and symptom data are personal health information.
