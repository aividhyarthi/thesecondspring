# Research Update Brief: What Changes in Body Composition During the Menopause Transition?

**Recommendation:** Add this research to the existing body-composition article and cross-link to the India heart-health article. Do not create a competing new URL.

## New research

Reyes H, Aggarwal B. "Changes in Body Composition During the Menopausal Transition and Cardiovascular Health Implications: A Contemporary Review." *Current Obstetrics and Gynecology Reports*. Published 23 November 2026. DOI: [10.1007/s13669-026-00503-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13669-026-00503-y).

The review included 16 studies published between 2021 and 2026 and considered muscle, adiposity, bone and cardiovascular health.

## What the review found

The strongest evidence for a menopause-specific body-composition change was for regional fat distribution, particularly visceral and android fat. Longitudinal data using the final menstrual period as a reference point showed accelerated accumulation during the menopausal transition, followed by slower gain after menopause.

The evidence for lower lean or skeletal muscle mass in postmenopausal groups was less conclusive about how much change was specifically due to menopause rather than chronological ageing. Bone mineral density also declined around the typical age of menopause, but the effects of ageing and menopause were difficult to separate.

The review included studies in which resistance training improved muscle and abdominal adiposity outcomes. Findings for hormone therapy, sleep and diet varied by outcome.

Most studies assessed cardiovascular risk factors rather than clinical cardiovascular events. The review therefore does not establish that a particular body-composition change causes a heart attack or that a specific intervention prevents cardiovascular events.

## Suggested new section for the existing article

### The evidence is strongest for changes in where fat is stored

A contemporary review published in November 2026 examined 16 studies of body composition during midlife and menopause. It found the clearest menopause-specific evidence for an increase in central and visceral fat during the menopause transition.

The findings for muscle and bone need more careful interpretation. Lower lean or skeletal muscle mass and declining bone mineral density were observed in some studies, but researchers could not always separate the effects of menopause from those of getting older.

This distinction matters because the menopause transition happens at the same time as other age-related changes. Not every change in strength, weight or bone density can be attributed to hormones alone.

## Suggested evidence-versus-uncertainty table

| What the review supports | What remains uncertain |
|---|---|
| Longitudinal evidence shows accelerated visceral and android fat accumulation during the menopausal transition. | The size of this change for every woman or every population. |
| Lower lean or skeletal muscle mass is reported in postmenopausal groups. | How much of the difference is caused by menopause rather than chronological ageing. |
| Bone mineral density declines around the typical age of menopause. | The separate contribution of menopause and ageing in all populations. |
| Resistance training improved muscle or abdominal adiposity outcomes in several included trials. | The best programme for every woman or whether it prevents cardiovascular events. |
| Visceral fat is the body-composition measure with the strongest link to cardiovascular risk factors in this review. | Whether body-composition changes directly cause clinical cardiovascular events. |

## India-specific context to add

The review is not an India-specific study. Do not present its findings as measurements of Indian women.

The article can explain that women in India may have different diets, activity patterns, healthcare access and chronic disease profiles. More Indian longitudinal research is needed to understand how body composition changes across the menopause transition in different communities.

The patient-facing section should focus on discussing health in a broader way with a clinician. Depending on individual history, this may include blood pressure, blood glucose, cholesterol, waist measurement, strength, physical activity and bone health. Do not prescribe a universal test panel or weight target.
