# The Second Spring Editorial Illustration System

## Visual identity
Use original hand-drawn pencil and light-watercolour illustrations inspired by the supplied reference image. The artwork should feel warm, human, Indian and editorial rather than clinical or like generic stock photography.

## Core art direction
- Clean white or warm-white background with generous negative space.
- Coloured-pencil linework, visible fine strokes and restrained watercolour shading.
- South Asian women mainly in their 40s, 50s and 60s, represented with varied skin tones, body types, hair textures, clothing and expressions.
- Indian everyday details where relevant: sarees, kurtas, home interiors, local foods, plants, family and work settings. Avoid making every image look identical or stereotyped.
- Muted palette: cream, terracotta, rust, warm brown, soft sage and muted rose.
- No text embedded in the image, no fake medical diagrams, no dramatic disease imagery, no logos generated inside artwork.
- Landscape 16:9 composition for article hero images. Keep the main subject clear at thumbnail size.
- Each article receives its own scene, props and composition based on its actual subject. Do not reuse one generic woman illustration across unrelated topics.

## Subject mapping
- Symptoms: woman experiencing the specific symptom in a calm, respectful everyday setting.
- Hormones and treatment: woman speaking with a qualified clinician, medicine packaging only when needed and never branded.
- Nutrition: culturally recognisable Indian food ingredients, shown accurately and without implying a cure.
- Fitness and muscle: realistic movement, strength and mobility, not unrealistic gym imagery.
- Heart and metabolic health: subtle, accurate health cues, not frightening anatomical graphics.
- Relationships, work and emotional wellbeing: natural human interactions and lived environments.

## Accessibility and publishing
Every image must have descriptive alt text explaining the scene and its editorial relevance. Avoid keyword stuffing. Use descriptive filenames and compress to WebP or optimised JPEG before production. Store production assets in `public/images/blog/` and reference them through the existing `image` and `imageAlt` frontmatter fields. Do not set `featured: true` unless editorially selected.

## Rollout
1. Start with the new research articles and existing high-value pages that currently have no image.
2. Audit all 450 current blog markdown files for missing, weak or duplicated images.
3. Prioritise high-traffic evergreen pages, symptom explainers, treatment guides and Indian-context articles.
4. Create one image per article, then add its image path and alt text to frontmatter.
5. Check desktop, mobile, article cards, social previews, image dimensions, compression and page speed before broad rollout.

## Quality control
A human editor should check that the image matches the article, represents women respectfully, contains no misleading medical detail and does not imply a treatment or supplement works unless the article's evidence supports that claim.
