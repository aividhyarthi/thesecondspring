# Editorial Update Brief: ACOG's 2026 Menopause Symptom Guideline

**Action:** Update the existing article at `/blog/acog-menopause-symptom-guideline`. Do not publish a second article targeting the same guideline.

## Why update now?

The existing article already covers ACOG Clinical Practice Guideline No. 13, published in November 2026. The full guideline provides more detail than a general announcement. The page should be strengthened with a clear, accurate summary of the recommendation strength and evidence quality.

## Suggested new section: What does ACOG recommend?

ACOG's 2026 guideline addresses vasomotor symptoms such as hot flashes and night sweats, related sleep disturbance and mood symptoms in people experiencing spontaneous or induced menopause from age 40 onwards.

The guideline recommends systemic menopausal hormone therapy for vasomotor symptoms in people without contraindications. For someone with a uterus, the regimen generally includes estrogen and a progestogen; estrogen alone may be used when endometrial protection is not needed. ACOG also recommends systemic hormone therapy for sleep disturbance associated with vasomotor symptoms in eligible individuals.

For people who do not want hormone therapy or have a contraindication, ACOG recommends SSRIs or SNRIs and neurokinin receptor antagonists for vasomotor symptoms and vasomotor-related sleep disturbance. Gabapentin is suggested when other recommended non-hormonal medicines are ineffective or poorly tolerated.

The guideline also suggests cognitive behavioural therapy for vasomotor symptoms, sleep disturbance and mood symptoms. Mindfulness-based interventions and physical exercise are suggested for sleep and mood symptoms. Clinical hypnosis and acupuncture receive conditional recommendations with lower-quality evidence.

These are guideline recommendations, not instructions for an individual reader to start a medicine or therapy. The strength of recommendation and evidence quality differ by intervention.

## Important context to retain

- The guideline does not say hormone therapy is suitable for every woman.
- Treatment decisions should account for age, timing, symptoms, medical history, contraindications, route, formulation and individual preferences.
- The guideline notes that hormone therapy may help sleep when sleep disturbance occurs alongside vasomotor symptoms. Evidence is less clear for sleep problems without vasomotor symptoms.
- Depression that meets criteria for major depressive disorder, or is the predominant symptom, should be assessed and treated using depression care guidance. Hormone therapy should not be presented as a substitute for appropriate depression treatment.
- Persistent or unscheduled bleeding after starting hormone therapy needs clinical evaluation.
- The guideline is from a US professional organisation. Explain that Indian clinicians should interpret it alongside Indian Menopause Society guidance and local prescribing practice.

5. When should we review the treatment?
