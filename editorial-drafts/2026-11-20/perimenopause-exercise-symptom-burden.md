# Can Exercise Make Perimenopause Easier? What the Research Really Says

Exercise is often recommended during midlife for heart health, bone strength, mood and sleep. Researchers are also asking whether physical activity may be associated with the overall burden of perimenopause symptoms.

A study announced by The Menopause Society in October 2026 used data from 1,808 Flo app users across 87 countries. The researchers reported that women who were more physically active tended to report a lower burden of perimenopause symptoms.

## What did the study suggest?

The analysis looked at reported activity frequency and symptom burden. The researchers observed that even some activity was associated with a lower symptom burden than reporting no physical activity.

That is an encouraging finding, particularly because it may make movement feel more achievable for women who cannot fit long workouts into a busy week. But the result needs careful interpretation.

## Association is not proof

The study was based on app-user data and was observational. It cannot establish that exercise itself caused symptoms to improve. Women who are more active may differ in sleep, general health, income, stress, medical care or other factors that also affect symptoms.

The study also does not establish a minimum exercise dose that will reliably reduce hot flashes, night sweats or other symptoms for every woman. The findings should not be presented as a treatment guarantee.

## Why movement still matters in midlife

Physical activity has established benefits beyond menopause symptoms. Depending on the type and intensity, it can support cardiovascular fitness, muscle strength, balance, bone health, mood and sleep. These are important areas of health as women move through midlife.

A balanced routine may include walking or another activity that raises the heart rate, strength-building exercises and balance work. The right starting point depends on a person's health, mobility, symptoms and previous activity levels.

## A realistic approach for Indian women

A formal gym membership is not necessary for everyone. Brisk walking, dancing, cycling, or suitable home-based strength exercises can be ways to build activity into daily life. The important thing is to choose an activity that is safe, accessible and sustainable.

Women with chest pain, unexplained breathlessness, dizziness, significant joint problems, osteoporosis or known heart disease should seek medical advice before starting or increasing exercise. Anyone with severe or disruptive menopause symptoms should also know that effective medical and non-medical treatments exist; exercise does not need to be the only response.

## What should women take away?

The research adds to the case for staying active during perimenopause, but it does not prove that a few exercise sessions will treat symptoms. Think of movement as one part of a wider plan that may include sleep support, nutrition, symptom assessment and a discussion with a healthcare professional.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can walking reduce hot flashes?**

The study found an association between physical activity and lower overall symptom burden. It did not prove that walking specifically reduces hot flashes.

**Do I need to exercise every day?**

The study does not establish a guaranteed frequency for symptom relief. Choose a sustainable activity plan based on general health guidance and individual ability.

**Can exercise replace menopause treatment?**

No. Exercise supports general health but should not be treated as a substitute for clinical assessment or evidence-based treatment when symptoms are troublesome.
