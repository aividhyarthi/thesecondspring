# What Can the Gut Microbiome of Centenarians Teach Us About Healthy Ageing?

People who live beyond 100 often attract attention for their diets, daily routines and family histories. Scientists are also studying another part of the picture: the trillions of microbes living in the gut and the chemical work they perform.

A study announced by the University of California San Diego on 16 November 2026 found a pattern of gut microbial activity in centenarians. Researchers then used engineered bacteria to amplify a related activity in aged mice, where they observed improvements in insulin sensitivity and other metabolic measures.

## What did researchers find in centenarians?

The team identified a microbial metabolic pattern involving the modification of bile acids. The researchers reported that this activity was associated with exceptional longevity in the human data they examined.

The finding is about microbial function, not simply which bacterial species were present. Two people can have different microbial communities that perform some similar functions, which is one reason scientists increasingly study what microbes do as well as which microbes are detected.

## What happened in the mouse experiments?

The researchers used engineered gut bacteria to increase the relevant activity in aged mice. The intervention improved insulin sensitivity and produced other favourable metabolic changes in the animals.

That is an important experimental step, but it is not evidence that the same approach works in people. Mouse biology does not always translate directly to human health, and engineered bacteria are not the same as an ordinary over-the-counter probiotic.

## Does this mean a probiotic can help people live to 100?

No. The study does not establish a probiotic product, food or supplement that extends human lifespan. It also does not show that changing one gut pathway will make someone live longer.

The human part of the research identified an association. The intervention was tested in aged mice. Human clinical trials would be needed to determine whether a safe intervention can produce meaningful health benefits in people.

## What can people do for gut health today?

A varied diet containing vegetables, fruits, pulses, whole grains, nuts and seeds can provide fibre that gut microbes use. In India, foods such as beans, lentils, vegetables and traditional fermented foods can be part of a varied diet, depending on tolerance and medical needs.

No single food guarantees a healthy microbiome. People with digestive disease, food allergies, kidney disease or other medical conditions may need individual dietary advice. Be cautious of products that claim to reset the microbiome or guarantee longevity.

## The takeaway

The study offers a fascinating clue about how gut microbes and metabolism may interact during ageing. Its most exciting intervention result is still in mice. For now, the practical lesson is to support overall metabolic health and avoid treating experimental microbiome research as a reason to buy an unproven supplement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do centenarians have a special gut microbiome?**

The study identified a microbial activity pattern associated with centenarians. It does not establish one universal microbiome shared by everyone who lives beyond 100.

**Can probiotics improve longevity?**

This study does not show that commercial probiotics extend human lifespan.

**Are fermented foods good for gut health?**

Some fermented foods can contribute to dietary variety, but effects differ by food and person. They are not a proven longevity treatment.
