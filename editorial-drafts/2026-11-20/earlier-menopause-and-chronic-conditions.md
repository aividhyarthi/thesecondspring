# Earlier Menopause and Long-Term Health: What a Study of Three Countries Found

For some women, menopause arrives earlier than expected. It may happen naturally, or it may follow surgery or medical treatment. A new study suggests that the age at which natural menopause occurs may offer doctors one clue about a woman's health later in life.

Published in *Frontiers in Public Health* on 16 November 2026, the research combined data from three long-running studies in the United States, England and Mexico. It included 6,576 women who had no more than one of seven specified chronic conditions at the start of follow-up.

## What did researchers find?

During an average follow-up of about 5.55 years, researchers recorded 2,031 new cases of multimorbidity. In this study, multimorbidity meant having at least two of the listed conditions.

Compared with women whose menopause occurred at ages 50 to 51, women who reported menopause at 45 or younger had a higher rate of subsequently reported multiple chronic conditions. The adjusted hazard ratios were 1.218 for menopause at 40 or younger and 1.214 for menopause between 41 and 45.

In plain language, the study found an association of roughly 21% higher relative hazard in those groups compared with the reference group. It does not mean that every woman who experiences early menopause has a 21% chance of developing multiple diseases.

## Which health conditions were considered?

The analysis considered high blood pressure, diabetes, cancer, chronic lung disease, heart disease, stroke, and arthritis or rheumatism. The outcome was the later reporting of at least two of these conditions.

This matters because the study was not looking at one disease alone. It was examining the accumulation of health problems over time.

## Does early menopause cause chronic disease?

The study cannot establish that. It was an analysis of observational cohort data, and health, social and reproductive factors may influence both the timing of menopause and later disease. The researchers adjusted for several factors and ran sensitivity analyses, but statistical adjustment cannot remove every possible source of bias.

The findings also should not be used to predict an individual's future. Menopause age is one part of a much larger health picture.

## What does this mean for women in India?

Women who experience menopause before 45 may benefit from discussing their longer-term health with a qualified clinician. The conversation can include blood pressure, blood glucose, cholesterol, bone health, smoking, family history, physical activity and any symptoms that need attention.

Menopause before 40 is generally considered premature ovarian insufficiency or premature menopause and deserves medical assessment. Menopause between 40 and 45 is often described as early menopause. These terms are not interchangeable, and a clinician can explain what applies in an individual case.

Women who have had their ovaries removed or whose ovarian function has been affected by cancer treatment need a different, individualised discussion. This study should not be used to make decisions about hormone therapy.

## The takeaway

Earlier menopause may be a useful marker that prompts a broader conversation about health in later life. It is not a verdict about what will happen. Good follow-up should focus on a woman's actual risk factors, symptoms and preventive care rather than fear about the age at which her periods stopped.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is considered early menopause?**

Menopause between ages 40 and 45 is commonly described as early menopause. Menopause before 40 is a separate clinical issue that requires assessment.

**Does early menopause mean I will develop diabetes or heart disease?**

No. The study found an association with a combined outcome of multiple chronic conditions. It cannot predict an individual's future or prove that menopause timing caused those conditions.

**Should women who experience early menopause have health checks?**

They should discuss an appropriate preventive health plan with a clinician. The checks depend on age, symptoms, family history and existing health conditions.
